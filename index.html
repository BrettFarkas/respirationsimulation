About the simplified model

The readouts show the whole cell’s production rates, regardless of the selected process tab. Percentages represent relative supply capacity, not measured concentrations. One fuel unit represents one glucose equivalent. This is a steady-state teaching model: changing a slider updates the modeled rates immediately. It does not simulate stored fuel, delayed responses, ATP demand, or cell death.

Glucose use G = glucose percentage / 100. Aerobic processing A = min(G, oxygen percentage / 100). The remaining fuel F = G − A follows glycolysis supported by lactate fermentation, which regenerates NAD⁺. ATP production = 2G + 30A; CO₂ production = 6A. Complete aerobic processing uses a simplified yield of 32 ATP per glucose (2 glycolysis + 2 Krebs equivalents + 28 oxidative phosphorylation); typical eukaryotic yields are approximately 30–32. Fermentation contributes no extra ATP beyond glycolysis and no CO₂ in this animal-cell model. Carbon is conserved: 6G = 6A in CO₂ + 6F in lactate. If glucose is absent, both rates are zero. At zero oxygen, glycolysis can still produce ATP when glucose is available.

The Krebs view includes pyruvate oxidation. The electron transport view combines the ETC and ATP synthase; oxygen is the final electron acceptor, and electron transport builds the proton gradient used to power ATP synthesis. Energy is transferred from fuel, not created. Other fuels and separate electron-carrier pools are omitted. Carbon dots in the splitting and breakdown sequences preserve the illustrated carbon count. The breakdown compresses several reactions and cycle turns; individual fuel carbons need not leave in the first Krebs turn. Overlapping packets repeat continuously; the entry and exit fades represent molecules entering and leaving the illustrated region, not matter disappearing. Other particles illustrate routes, not quantitative molecular counts. The rotating synthase is a schematic motor; its speed is illustrative, not an actual rotation rate. Glucose enters glycolysis; oxygen travels to the electron transport chain, not to glycolysis or the Krebs cycle. The whole-cell animation remains visible on every tab. The page stays still until Start Simulation is pressed. This explicit action enables continuous animation, including when the browser requests reduced motion. Animation pauses while this browser tab is hidden and resumes when it becomes visible.
